02A · Corpus & Training-Set Absence

Cryptographic Proofs of Absence Without Revealing the Dataset

Safety evaluators, regulators, and enterprise counterparties frequently need to verify a negative claim: “Prove that this confidential training corpus (or fine-tuning batch, or RAG store) contains zero occurrences of MMLU/GPQA benchmark test prompts, zero SSNs/API keys, or zero spans from a copyrighted repository—and prove you did not omit a single shard while checking.”

Why Naive Hashing Fails Absence Proofs

A standard Merkle inclusion proof can only show that a document is present at leaf $i$. Proving that a pattern $P$ is absent across an entire corpus $[0, N)$ requires preventing three distinct attacks simultaneously: (1) selective shard omission during the scan, (2) tokenization boundary evasion (splitting a forbidden string across BPE tokens), and (3) dishonest regex/CFG evaluation over the committed bytes. Our stack closes all three with machine-checked Lean 4 theorems and Plonky3 STARKs.

The 6-Phase End-to-End Absence Proof Protocol

01

Corpus Commitment & Contiguous MMR Indexing (dregg-doc + dregg-query)

Training shards, documents, or agent turns are packed into 8-felt Poseidon2 Merkle trees (dregg-doc/src/commit.rs:DocTree, domain tags 0x444f4330 / 0x444f4331) and appended to a Blake3Mmr or Poseidon2 MMR (dregg-query/src/lib.rs) where every entry’s 0-based position $i \in [0, N)$ is cryptographically bound into its leaf hash H(0x00 || u64_le(pos) || item_hash).

02

Non-Omission Range Certificate (MMR.lean:server_cannot_omit_position)

To prove that every shard in $[lo, hi]$ was scanned with zero gaps, Blake3Mmr::prove_range(lo, hi) emits a RangeCertificate containing only the $O(\log N)$ left-boundary and right-boundary sibling hashes. The verifier reconstructs all internal MMR subtrees from the contiguous slice and folds them against the bagged MMR root.

03

Certified Maximal-Munch BPE Tokenization (breadstuffs/crates/tokenizer)

A dishonest provider could hide a forbidden benchmark question by tokenizing it with non-standard subword splits. Our tokenizer/src/bpe.rs (prove_bpe_correctness) generates a STARK proof over the priority-ranked merge table proving that every adjacent token pair $(t_i, t_{i+1})$ has merge priority strictly worse than the merges that formed $t_i$ and $t_{i+1}$—enforcing canonical tokenization.

04

Lean-Verified Brzozowski Regex-Complement DFAs (Dregg2/Crypto/Deriv/* & dregg-dfa)

Given a forbidden pattern $R$ (e.g., an eval benchmark regex, PII class, or secret format), we compile its complement language $\Sigma^* \setminus \mathcal{L}(R)$ via Re::not() in breadstuffs/crates/dregg-dfa/src/regex.rs. Because standard NFA Thompson construction cannot be soundly complemented by flipping accepting states, we formalized a 3,522-line Lean 4 symbolic Brzozowski derivative engine with ACI normalization (metatheory/Dregg2/Crypto/Deriv/{Pred,RE,Deriv,Smart,Norm,Finiteness,DFA}.lean) proving language equivalence (correctness), state-space finiteness (der_finite), and exact complement disjointness (predRE_complement_disjoint).

05

Chomsky-Hierarchy Parsing Certificates & 8-Lane Poseidon2 Welding (Cfg.lean + AttestedAutomatonWeld8.lean)

For structured data (JSON schemas, tool call ASTs, code grammars), our hierarchy spans Type-3 DFAs (dfa-air, 7 columns), compact deterministic LL(1)/Dyck pushdown automata (CfgCompact.lean, DyckStackEmit.lean, 12 columns), and full Chomsky Normal Form CYK tables (Cfg.lean). AttestedAutomatonWeld8.lean welds the 8-byte DFA transition directly into the rate-8 Poseidon2 sponge absorption row—achieving 100% column savings on the automaton trace and proving 4M tokens in ~40 ms on a single thread.

06

Zero-Count Audit Receipt & 3-Level Recursive STARK Rollup (audit + turn-prover + DARK-TRAINING.md)

In breadstuffs/crates/audit/src/{count.rs,budget.rs}, setting limit = 0 in BudgetProof::prove forces the running match accumulator to remain identically 0 across every committed shard (`CountProof` with `match_count == 0`). `turn-prover` folds per-shard receipts through a 3-level Plonky3 recursive STARK tree (`RecursiveAggregation.lean:light_client_verifies_anchored_history`), and `zkml-research/docs/DARK-TRAINING.md` binds the resulting corpus root directly to the training run’s gradient updates inside the enclave.

02B · Selvage Architecture

Selvage: Commit-Then-Audit Workload Verification (`AuditSampling.lean`)

In weaving, a selvage is the self-finished edge of fabric that keeps the weave from unraveling without adding bulk to the cloth. In ~/dev/zkml-research, Selvage replaces per-token full-circuit zkML with a cryptographically binding commit-on-the-hot-path + VRF-sampled STARK audit protocol proven sound in Lean 4.

Hot Path · SynchronousLine Rate

Phase 1: Synchronous Boundary Commitment

During live inference, the provider commits to the input/output activation vectors and MoE routing gates of each chunk $i \in [1, N]$ in a Poseidon2 Merkle tree anchored to the model weight registry root $W_{\text{root}}$ and hardware TEE quote—before the random audit challenge seed is revealed.

Audit Path · Asynchronous78×–308× Reduction

Phase 2: VRF-Derived STARK Spot-Checks

After commitment, a VRF/Fiat-Shamir challenge selects each chunk independently with probability $q$ (or samples $k$ chunks without replacement). Any single cheated chunk is caught with probability $\ge 1 - (1 - q)^c$, while an adaptive adversary’s expected escape duration is bounded by $\mathbb{E}[C_\tau] \le 1/q$ via Doob’s Optional Stopping Theorem.

Native `MXFP4` Block-32 Dot Products

Instead of emulating IEEE-754 floats inside a field, we exploit the structure of OCP MXFP4 (E2M1): every 32-element block shares one `E8M0` scale byte, and the 16 E2M1 values times $2$ map to exact integers $\{0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \pm 3, \pm 4, \pm 6, \pm 8, \pm 12\}$. A 32-wide inner product fits in $\le 13$ bits—computed exactly in a single BabyBear ($p = 2013265921$) field word with zero rounding error!

In sparse MoE models (`gpt-oss-20b`: 24 layers, 32 experts, top-4 active), the router projection + top-k gate selection accounts for only 0.72% of active parameters. We prove router + attention (3.8% of params) in ZK and spot-audit linear FFN experts inside the TEE—delivering a 19× net verification speedup while preventing silent expert-dropping attacks.
